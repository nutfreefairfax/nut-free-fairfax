The list

Covers Fairfax County, the City of Fairfax, and the City of Falls Church, Virginia.

A quick note before you eat

This directory is for informational purposes only. It is not a guarantee of allergen safety or a substitute for medical advice. Continue following your healthcare provider’s recommendations.

Where the information comes from

Each listing reflects what restaurant or bakery staff shared with me by phone, email, or message on the date shown. I do not test or inspect kitchens. Menus, ingredients, and staff can change, so always contact the business to reconfirm before you go or order.

Understanding the two tabs

The first tab lists businesses whose staff confirmed that their kitchen is peanut and tree nut free. The second lists businesses that are not nut free but shared how they handle nut allergies. Nuts may still be present, and cross contact is possible.

Needs recheck means the information has not been reconfirmed within the past six months.

In an emergency, use your prescribed emergency medication as directed and call 911. Read the full disclaimer.

How I check

  1. 1

    I find the places

    I start with restaurants and bakeries around Fairfax County and see what they say online.

  2. 2

    I contact each place directly

    I ask whether they use or store peanuts and tree nuts in their food or kitchen, and whether they have a dedicated nut free or shared kitchen. A website alone is never enough. If they use nuts but explain how they handle allergy orders, I list them separately and clearly state that they are not nut free.

  3. 3

    I add the date

    Every listing shows when I last confirmed it. After 6 months it is marked Needs recheck until I confirm again.

Why I made this

Hi, I'm Jan.

I'm a mom in Fairfax, and my son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. Finding places where we can comfortably eat out together hasn't always been easy.

I often found myself searching Google, checking restaurant websites, and contacting places one by one. National allergy websites and apps can be helpful, but I wanted something more local, with information gathered directly from restaurants and bakeries and a clear date showing when I last confirmed each one.

That's why I created Nut Free Fairfax.

I personally reach out to restaurants and bakeries to ask about peanuts and tree nuts in their food, ingredients, and kitchen practices. I document what their staff share and include the date of each confirmation, so families can see when the information was last checked.

This directory is a work in progress. I'm continually reaching out to more local businesses and working to keep existing listings updated. The information comes directly from businesses and is not an independent inspection or a guarantee of safety. Families should always contact the business again before dining.

My hope is to make finding local dining options a little easier for other families navigating food allergies.
