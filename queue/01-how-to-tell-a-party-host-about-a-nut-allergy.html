The One Text To Send The Host Before The Party

Picture it.

It's Saturday, 2 pm. The bounce house is humming in the backyard like a tiny airplane. Kids are screaming the happy kind of screaming. The whole kitchen smells like pizza and frosting.

And someone hands your kid a cupcake. Pink sprinkles. Big smile.

Wait. What is in this?

Your stomach drops. Just a little. Just enough.

My son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. So yes, I think about this moment a lot.

Remember Home Alone? The McCallisters were already on the plane when they noticed Kevin was missing. Too late to fix it. That is the cupcake moment. The cupcake is already in the hand, and now you have to ask in front of fifteen people.

So here is what I do now. I ask early. One text, a few days before.

And I know what you are thinking. I don't want to be that parent.

Same. That little knot in your stomach is normal. But hear this: a host would so much rather get one friendly text on Wednesday than a worried face at the snack table on Saturday.

Here is a text you can copy:

Hi! We're so excited for Saturday. Quick heads up, [my son / I] have a peanut and tree nut allergy. Could you tell me what's on the menu, or save the packages so I can check the labels? No stress if not. I'm happy to bring our own food.

Short. Friendly. Easy to say yes to.

Why it works

It is a heads up, not a demand. The host does not have to change the whole menu.

It asks for something small. Packages, not a talk about cross contact.

It gives the host an easy out. When you offer to bring your own food, nobody has to promise anything they cannot promise.

Instead of "Is everything nut free?", ask "Can I see the package?" The first one is a guess. The second one you can check yourself.

If the answer is vague

Maybe you get "Oh, it's fine, there is nothing in it."

That one makes my stomach drop too.

Ask again: "Which brand is it? I'd love to check the label."

Still not sure: Skip that dish. Eat before you go, or bring your own.

You are not rude for asking. You are the one who has to live with the answer.

So next party, picture it again. The bounce house hums. The cupcake shows up. And you already know what is in it. Your shoulders drop. You grab a slice of pizza and actually enjoy the noise.

One text. That is all it took.
