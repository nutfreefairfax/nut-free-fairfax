The Text I Send Every Host

It's Wednesday night. The invite is on your phone. Saturday, 2 pm, a birthday party.

You open a new message to the host. Your thumb just sits there.

Do I really have to ask?

Your stomach tightens. Just a little.

My son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. So yes, I have sat in front of that blank message more than once.

And I know what you are thinking. I don't want to be that parent.

Same. My first text felt awkward. Then the host wrote back that they would make something for my son.

That is how it usually goes. A host would so much rather get one friendly text on Wednesday than a worried face at the snack table on Saturday.

Here is a text you can copy:

Hi! We're so excited for Saturday. Quick heads up, [my son / I] have a peanut and tree nut allergy. Could you let me know what's on the menu, or save the packages so I can check the labels? No stress if not. I'm happy to bring our own food.

Short. Friendly. Easy to say yes to.

If the answer is vague

Maybe you get "Oh, it's fine, there is nothing in it."

That one makes my stomach tighten too. Ask again: "Which brand is it? I'd love to check the label."

Still not sure: Skip that dish. Eat before you go, or bring your own.

You are not rude for asking. You are the one who has to live with the answer.

The part I won't skip

Bringing your own food has a cost. At first I'm okay. Then I watch the other kids eat the party food, and my son can't. That part makes me feel bad.

But it is still better than finding out at the snack table.

So send the text. Hit send before you talk yourself out of it.

Then Saturday comes. The bounce house hums. You already know what is in the cupcake. Your shoulders drop. You grab one, pink sprinkles and all, then a slice of pizza, and actually enjoy the noise.

One text. That is all it took.
