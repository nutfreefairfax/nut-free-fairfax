The One Text To Send The Host Before The Party

One short text. No awkward talk.

The invite comes in. Pizza, cake, a bounce house. And you are already wondering what is in all of it.

You do not want to be the awkward guest. You also cannot skip the question.

So do not make it a big talk. Send one text.

Send it early: A few days before works best. The host still has time to check labels, and nobody is stressed at the door.

Here is the text:

Hi! We're so excited for Saturday. Quick heads up, [my son / I] have a peanut and tree nut allergy. Could you tell me what's on the menu, or save the packages so I can check the labels? No stress if not. I'm happy to bring our own food.

Short. Friendly. Easy to say yes to.

Why this works

Three reasons.

It is a heads up, not a demand. The host does not have to change the whole menu.

It asks for something small. Packages, not a talk about cross contact.

It gives the host an easy out. When you offer to bring your own food, nobody has to promise anything they can not promise.

Instead of "Is everything nut free?", ask "Can I see the package?" The first one is a guess. The second one you can check yourself.

If the answer is vague

Sometimes you get "Oh, it's fine, there is nothing in it."

Ask again: "Which brand is it? I'd love to check the label."

Still not sure: Skip that one dish. Eat before you go, or bring your own.

Remember: You are not rude for asking. You are the one who has to live with the answer.

The invite came in. You sent one text. Now the only thing left to figure out is the gift.
