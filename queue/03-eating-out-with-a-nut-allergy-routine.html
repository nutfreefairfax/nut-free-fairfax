Before We Order: My 3 Step Nut Allergy Routine

Three steps. That is the whole routine.

"It's allergy free."

A server told us that once. It wasn't.

I'm not saying this to scare you. It's just why I do things the way I do now.

My son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. Before we eat out, I follow three steps.

This is what works for me, not medical advice. Please follow your allergist's guidance for your family.

My 3 steps

Step 1, before we leave: I look at the menu online. Then I call. I ask if they cater to nut allergies, or if they have an allergy menu.

One call tells me a lot. And it saves us from sitting down at a place that can't help.

Step 2, when we order: I ask for the allergy menu. I tell the server about my son's allergy. Then I ask, "What would you suggest for him?"

I don't just point at the menu and hope.

Step 3, if something feels off: I don't give him the food. At all.

No "maybe just a bite." No debate.

Instead of "It's probably fine," my rule is this: if I'm not sure, he doesn't eat it.

That is the whole routine. Call first. Ask at the table. Say no when it feels wrong.

Funny thing is, it all started with that one sentence, "It's allergy free." Now I ask for more than a promise. I ask for the allergy menu, the details, and I listen to my gut.

Want a head start? Every place on my list told me directly about their kitchen. Start there, then do your own checking.
