“We’re careful with allergies” tells you nothing. Ask this instead.

Three bakeries. One question. Three totally different answers.

I called three bakeries in Fairfax and asked each one the same thing: do you use or store peanuts or tree nuts?

The first one said no. Not anywhere in the kitchen.

The second one uses walnuts and pistachios, but bakes allergy orders separately.

The third keeps nuts in a storage room, away from the kitchen.

Any of them could have said, "Oh, we're careful with allergies." And that sentence tells you almost nothing.

I'm a mom in Fairfax, and my son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. So here's how I ask, to get a real answer.

This is what works for me, not medical advice. Please follow your allergist's guidance for your family.

The questions I ask

Question 1: Do you use or store peanuts or tree nuts in your kitchen? Use and storage are two different things. A bag in the back room is not the same as nuts in the recipes.

Question 2: Is the whole kitchen nut free, or is it shared? This tells me if nuts touch the same counters, ovens, or equipment.

Question 3: How do you handle allergy orders? I ask this when a place does use nuts. A real answer sounds like baking allergy orders separately or checking ingredient labels. "We can accommodate" with no details doesn't count for me.

Question 4: Which nuts do you use? Peanuts and tree nuts aren't the same, and your family may need to avoid one or both. Ask about the ones that matter to you.

Instead of asking "Is this nut free?", ask what they use and what they store. You'll get a much better answer.

Make the call easier

Call at a slow time: mid afternoon is usually calmer than lunch or dinner.

Ask for a manager: or the person who bakes or cooks. They know the kitchen best.

Keep it short: I say who I am and that I have two quick questions. People help so much more when they know it's quick.

Write it down: who you talked to, what they said, and the date. Menus and staff change.

Ask again before each visit: what was true last month might not be true today.

If it doesn't feel right

Trust that.

If someone sounds unsure, rushed, or like they're guessing, I hear that as "not today." Say thank you and move on. You don't owe anyone an explanation.

Three bakeries. Three answers. Now you know what to ask.

Want a head start? Every place on my list told me directly about their kitchen. Start there, then do your own checking.
