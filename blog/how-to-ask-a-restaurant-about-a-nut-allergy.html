How to ask a restaurant about a nut allergy

Posted November 24, 2026

I'm a mom in Fairfax, and my son has a peanut and tree nut allergy. I contact restaurants and bakeries all the time for this site. Here are the questions I ask, in the order I ask them.

This is what works for me, not medical advice. Please follow your allergist's guidance for your family.

Why "nut free" is not one thing

When a place says "nut free," it can mean a few different things. Maybe the whole kitchen has no peanuts or tree nuts at all. Maybe there are no nuts in the recipes, but nuts are in the building. Maybe they only mean no peanuts. The words sound the same, so I ask more questions to find out which one it is.

My questions, in order

  1. "Do you use or store peanuts or tree nuts in your kitchen?" I ask about use and storage, because a bag in a back room is different from nuts in the recipes.
  2. "Is the whole kitchen nut free, or is it shared?" This tells me if nuts are handled on the same counters, ovens, or equipment.
  3. "How do you handle allergy orders?" I ask this when a place does use nuts. A real answer is something like baking allergy orders separately or checking ingredient labels. A vague "we can accommodate" is not enough for me.
  4. "Which nuts do you use?" Peanuts and tree nuts are not the same, and your family may need to avoid one or both. Ask about the specific nuts that matter for you.

Tips that make the call easier

  • Call at a slow time. Mid afternoon is usually calmer than lunch or dinner.
  • Ask for a manager or the person who bakes or cooks. They usually know the kitchen best.
  • Start friendly and short. I say who I am and that I have two quick questions. People are much more helpful when they know it will be quick.
  • Write down the date and who you spoke with. Menus and staff change, so it helps to know when you last checked.
  • Ask again before each visit. What was true last month may not be true today.

If the answer does not feel right

If someone sounds unsure, rushed, or guesses, I treat that as a no for now. It's okay to thank them and move on. You never have to explain yourself.
