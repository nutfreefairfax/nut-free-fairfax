How to ask a restaurant about a nut allergy

Posted November 24, 2026

Asking a stranger about your kid's allergy can feel awkward. You're not being difficult. You're being a good parent. I'm a mom in Fairfax, my son has a peanut and tree nut allergy, and I make these calls for this site all the time. Here's exactly what I ask, so you can skip the awkward and get your answer.

This is what works for me, not medical advice. Please follow your allergist's guidance for your family.

First, "nut free" is not one thing

Here's the thing. Two places can both say "nut free" and mean totally different things. One might have a kitchen with no peanuts or tree nuts at all. Another might leave nuts out of the recipes, but keep them in the building. Another might only mean no peanuts.

Same words. Very different kitchens. That's why I never stop at the first answer.

What I ask, in this order

  1. "Do you use or store peanuts or tree nuts in your kitchen?" I ask about use and storage. A bag in the back room is not the same as nuts in the recipes.
  2. "Is the whole kitchen nut free, or is it shared?" This tells me if nuts touch the same counters, ovens, or equipment.
  3. "How do you handle allergy orders?" I ask this when a place does use nuts. A real answer sounds like baking allergy orders separately or checking ingredient labels. A vague "we can accommodate" doesn't count for me.
  4. "Which nuts do you use?" Peanuts and tree nuts aren't the same, and your family may need to avoid one or both. Ask about the ones that matter to you.

Make the call easier on yourself

  • Call at a slow time. Mid afternoon is usually calmer than lunch or dinner.
  • Ask for a manager, or the person who bakes or cooks. They know the kitchen best.
  • Keep it short and friendly. I say who I am and that I have two quick questions. People help so much more when they know it's quick.
  • Write down the date and who you talked to. Menus and staff change, so you'll want to know when you last checked.
  • Ask again before each visit. What was true last month might not be true today.

If the answer doesn't feel right

Trust that. If someone sounds unsure, rushed, or like they're guessing, I treat it as a "not today." Say thank you and move on. You never owe anyone an explanation.

That's why I made this list. Every place on it told me directly about their kitchen, and I show the date I confirmed it. Use it as your starting point, then do your own checking before you go.
